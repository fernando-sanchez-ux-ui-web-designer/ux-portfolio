The Yiros Shop - Bringing the new brand online

Rebuilt a 10 year old WordPress presence into a mobile first Webflow site that matched a premium rebrand, prioritized menu and store.

Role

UX/UI Designer & Webflow Developer

Timeline

6 Months

Tools

Webflow, Illustrator, Photoshop, Figma, Zapier, Redcat (Integration)

01. The Challenge(Context)

The Yiros Shop is a staple Greek fast-food franchise in Brisbane, Australia, with over 15 years of history. In 2024, the brand underwent a massive identity shift, redesigning their physical stores, packaging, and social media presence. However, their digital footprint was obsolete—a 10-year-old WordPress site that didn't reflect their new premium "vibe."

The Problem:

The original site was not responsive, lacked information hierarchy, and provided a poor user experience. My mission was to translate the new physical brand manual into a vibrant, high-converting digital experience.

02. The Process: From Insight to Strategy

While my initial role in the company wasn't web design, my deep understanding of their operations allowed me to act as the bridge between the CEO's vision and the user's needs.

Discovery & Research Phase

  • Heuristic Evaluation: I performed a deep dive into the legacy site, identifying friction points and a lack of clear conversion goals.
  • CMS Consulting: After analyzing various platforms, I recommended Webflow over WordPress to provide the marketing team with autonomy and superior site performance.
  • Stakeholder Interviews: I interviewed the CEO, Marketing, and Operations managers to align the digital strategy with the franchise's real-world operational needs.
  • Competitive Benchmarking: I analyzed the Australian fast-food industry patterns, identifying UI standards and common UX failures in competitors to find our "gap" in the market.

Information Architecture (IA)

I designed a 6-month roadmap, dedicating the first month strictly to planning and IA.

  • Conversion Focus: We identified the Menu and the Store Locator as the most critical pages for ROI.
  • Third-Party Integration UX: Since the checkout process was outsourced to Redcat, I focused on simplifying the pre-ordering user journey to minimize drop-offs and ensure a frictionless transition to the payment gateway.

03. Execution: Design & Build

I moved from paper sketches to low-fidelity wireframes, followed by a dynamic Moodboard to gain stakeholder buy-in on the visual direction before high-fidelity production.

  • Hi-Fi Prototyping

    I built interactive prototypes in Figma, integrating new professional photography and brand assets. I collaborated closely with a graphic designer to ensure every asset was pixel-perfect and on-brand.

  • Webflow Development

    I developed the site with a mobile-first approach, ensuring the "Greek soul" of the brand was felt through interactions and clean layouts.

  • Operational Automation

    I implemented Zapier workflows to connect the website with internal franchise and operations departments, automating lead generation and data handling.

Wireframing & Iteration

The design process was far from linear; it was a constant evolution driven by functionality. I started with hand-drawn sketches to quickly explore layout possibilities without getting distracted by aesthetics. This allowed me to validate the information architecture with stakeholders early on.

04. The Results

The launch was a pivotal success, synchronizing the digital experience with the brand's physical overhaul.

  • Visual Impact: A modern, responsive website that perfectly aligns with the new store interiors and packaging.
  • Operational Efficiency: The marketing team can now manage content independently, while the operations team receives automated, real-time data.
  • Stakeholder Success: The project exceeded the expectations of the CEO and Marketing Manager, solidifying the brand's digital authority in the Brisbane market.

05. Key Learnings & Reflections

•

Working within a mid-sized franchise structure taught me that communication is just as vital as the design itself. I learned to manage complex timelines and expectations, recognizing that project delays are often opportunities to refine team synchronization and pivot strategically.

•

A pivotal part of my growth was learning to detach my personal bias from my projects. I realized that a "great idea" is only as good as its ability to withstand feedback and critical analysis. I mastered the art of professional introspection, turning constructive criticism into actionable improvements for the final product. I learned that strong information hierarchy often beats extra content, especially for hungry, time pressured users.

•

I improved how I translate stakeholder vision into user centered decisions by grounding conversations in page goals and journeys.

•

I deepened my understanding of designing around third party checkout constraints while still protecting trust and conversion.

Business Impact

The new Webflow site established a digital presence that finally matched The Yiros Shop's 2024 brand upgrade, helping the business look as premium online as it does in-store. By prioritizing menu and store discovery, the site supported faster decisions and clearer paths into ordering, which directly supports revenue goals. Internally, the marketing team gained autonomy to publish and update content without developer bottlenecks, and automation reduced manual handling for operations. The project also strengthened stakeholder confidence by turning a vague desire for a better website into a measurable, conversion-focused digital strategy.
